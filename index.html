01 · The problem and the person

A plan at 6. The outfit still at home.

Pronto is designed first for Dublin women aged 18–35 who decide to go out or attend an evening event with only a few hours to get ready. The initial service area and customer age are assumptions to validate.

An invented customer quote
“I have the invite, but I don’t have time to go around town looking for something to wear.”
Illustrative wording only. No customer interview has been completed yet.

Today, someone short on time may search several fashion websites, message friends, or travel to a shop and hope the right size is there. Standard online shopping does not answer the immediate question: can I find the right look in my size and have it before I leave?

What a useful service must do
Show evening and occasion looks in one quick edit.
Show sizes and sample availability before choosing.
Make the estimated arrival time visible beside each look.
Let a phone user reserve a look in a few taps.
Include a formal menswear edit: suits, shirts, selected jeans, designer tees and polos, ties and cufflinks.
Show item and delivery prices before the user reserves.

02 · The solution

Three steps from invite to outfit.

Pronto is a proposed mobile-first local fashion marketplace. It would connect selected local stock with a rapid courier service. The demo simulates the browsing and reservation flow only.

Prototype only. Items, stock, prices, delivery minutes and reservation references are made-up sample data. Arrival estimates are calculated from the current time; they are not courier quotes. Reservation details stay in this browser’s local storage. No payment, message or delivery is created.

Alpha test completed on a phone

A tester who did not build the page found a women’s night-out outfit in size 10 for under €80 and made a sample reservation. They liked the concept once they reached the demo, but found the page text-heavy and the original outfit shapes unclear. In response, the detailed requirements were placed in an expandable section, the demo moved saved reservations above the catalogue, and the outfit placeholders were redesigned with brighter, dressing-room-inspired colours and clearer sample-illustration labels.
